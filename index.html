Macro Fundamentals

How fundamentals-based prediction actually works

There are only three real mechanisms behind any "they knew the number before it came out" claim — this panel uses the one that's genuinely free and fetches it live instead of asking you to type numbers in.

The three real mechanisms
1. Survey consensus — outlets like Trading Economics, Econoday, and Bloomberg poll dozens of economists before each release and publish the median guess (e.g. "CPI forecast: 3.1%"). This is a licensed, paid product — there is no free, keyless API that publishes it.

2. Market-priced expectations — prices already baked into other instruments reveal what the market collectively expects: Treasury yields and TIPS breakevens price in inflation expectations, Fed funds futures price in rate-decision odds, and crypto funding/options skew price in positioning. Some of this is free; this desk already uses the crypto side of it (funding rate, PCR, skew) everywhere else on the site.

3. Trend / momentum nowcasting — extrapolating the next print from the recent trend in actual released data. It's genuinely free to compute from free historical data, but it is not a forecast in the economist-consensus sense — it just continues the recent pattern, which real releases break constantly.
This panel below runs #3 for you automatically, live, from free data — no manual entry. #1 stays a paid product (flagged honestly, not faked); #2 is partially covered via the 10-Year Treasury yield card as a genuine market-priced reference point.
Live macro data — CPI, unemployment, Fed funds rate, 10Y yield Idle

1. Terms & abbreviations

Sign in to view the full glossary.

What every label on this site actually means.

OTM (out-of-the-money)
A call with a strike above current price, or a put with a strike below it. No intrinsic value yet — the premium is pure time/volatility value, which is why OTM options are cheaper and need a bigger move to pay off.
ITM (in-the-money)
A call with a strike below current price, or a put above it. Already has intrinsic value, so it costs more and moves closer to 1:1 with the underlying.
ATM (at-the-money)
Strike approximately equal to current price. Maximum time value, maximum sensitivity to a move in either direction — the typical straddle strike.
Breakeven
The underlying price at expiry where the structure's P&L crosses zero. Debit structures (straddle, strangle) usually have two breakevens; credit structures (iron condor) also have two, marking the edges of the profit zone.
Max profit / max loss
The best and worst outcomes the structure can produce at expiry, given the strikes and premiums you entered. For defined-risk structures both are capped; for long straddles/strangles max loss is capped at premium paid but max profit is theoretically open. "Uncapped" means the model has no ceiling to show you — treat it as large, not infinite in practice.
Net debit / net credit
Debit = you pay to open (buying costs more than what you sold). Credit = you collect to open (selling brings in more than what you bought). Shown under "Net debit/credit" in the builder results.
Qty (ratio)
How many contracts of that specific leg relative to the others — e.g. a ratio spread sells 2 contracts for every 1 it buys. This is per-leg, not the overall position size.
Multiplier
Contract size. Crypto options on Deribit are 1 coin per contract (multiplier 1); equity options are typically 100 shares per contract (multiplier 100). Get this wrong and every dollar figure in the builder is wrong by that factor.
Theta (time decay)
How much an option's value erodes per day, all else equal. Long premium structures (straddle, strangle, backspreads, guts) bleed theta — that's the "Long Vol" badge. Short premium structures (condor, butterfly, calendars, ratio spreads, ladder) collect it — the "Short Vol" badge. "Flat" (box spread) means theta is a side-effect, not the point.
Delta-neutral
A combined position whose net directional sensitivity is approximately zero. Institutional desks build and continuously rebalance delta-neutral books; this builder gives you a static snapshot at entry, not a rebalancing engine.
Implied volatility (IV)
The market's priced-in expectation of future movement, baked into the option's premium right now. Read off Deribit's at-the-money, nearest-expiry option via mark_iv.
Realized volatility (RV)
How much the underlying has actually moved recently (Deribit's own historical volatility series). The whole "cheap vs rich premium" read is just IV minus RV.
IV − RV spread ("vol premium")
Implied minus realized, in volatility points. Positive means the market is pricing in more movement than has actually been happening (premium looks rich — favors selling it). Negative means implied is running below what's actually occurring (premium looks cheap — favors buying it).
Funding rate
The periodic payment perpetual-futures longs and shorts make to each other, here read every 8 hours. Persistently positive = longs paying shorts (crowd leaning long); persistently negative = the reverse. Used here only as a crowding/stretch signal, not a funding-carry trade.
Fear & Greed Index (FNG)
A 0–100 sentiment score (alternative.me). Low = fear, high = greed. Extremes in either direction (≤20 or ≥80) are read here as "more likely to produce a sharp move," which cuts for long-vol structures and against pinned/range structures.
Put/Call OI ratio (PCR)
Total open interest in puts divided by total open interest in calls, across the option chain (Deribit get_book_summary_by_currency). Near 1.0 is balanced positioning. Well above 1 means puts are crowded (downside hedging/betting dominant); well below means calls are crowded.
IV skew (put − call)
Average implied vol of puts minus average implied vol of calls across the chain. Positive skew (puts pricier) is the normal/defensive state in most markets; a flip or a big widening is itself informative about which side is paying up for protection.
Total option OI
Total open contracts across the whole chain — a rough proxy for how much size and liquidity is actually sitting in the options market right now, separate from direction.
BTC mempool congestion
Unconfirmed-transaction backlog and the fastest recommended fee on the Bitcoin network (mempool.space). Shown only for BTC — a crude on-chain activity/urgency gauge, nothing more.
BTC dominance
Bitcoin's share of total crypto market cap (CoinGecko). Rising dominance usually means capital rotating toward BTC and away from alts (risk-off within crypto); falling dominance usually means the opposite.
GO / CAUTION / AVOID
The desk's shorthand verdict per structure. GO = the live signals line up with what that structure needs to work. CAUTION = no strong edge either way — the math still works, but nothing above is doing you a favor. AVOID = the live signals are actively working against this specific structure's weak point.
"Crowded" positioning
This desk's label for PCR above 1.3 or below 0.6 — positioning lopsided enough that a squeeze back the other way is a real risk, which is specifically bad for anything short premium with a tail (condor, butterfly, ratio spreads, ladder).
Economist survey consensus
The "expected" number you see quoted before a CPI, NFP, or other macro release (e.g. "CPI forecast: 3.1%"). Compiled by polling economists at banks and funds. This is a paid product (Trading Economics, Econoday, Bloomberg) — no free keyless API publishes it.
Market-priced expectation
What a forward-looking price already implies about the future — a bond yield pricing in expected inflation, a futures curve pricing in an expected rate move, a funding rate pricing in crowd positioning. Not a guess; it's what the market is already paying for.
Desk trend estimate ("next print")
This desk's own naive extrapolation in the Macro Fundamentals panel — the average of the last 3 month-over-month moves in an actual released series, carried forward one step. It is not a survey consensus and does not claim to be — real releases break trend constantly.

3. How to actually read a signal, in plain terms

Sign in to view the full Q&A.

IV is above realized vol — what do I do with that?
Options are pricing in more movement than has actually been happening. That's good for strategies that sell premium (condor, butterfly, calendars, ratio spreads, ladder) and bad for strategies that buy it (straddle, strangle, backspreads, guts) — you'd be overpaying for movement that hasn't shown up yet.
IV is below realized vol — what do I do with that?
The reverse: options are cheap relative to what's actually been moving. Favors buying premium (straddle, strangle, backspreads, guts, reverse condor) and is a weaker setup for selling it, since you'd be collecting less than the recent movement would justify.
Fear & Greed is at an extreme — what does that change?
Extremes (fear or greed) have historically preceded sharper reversals or continuation moves than a neutral reading. That raises the odds a big-move (long-vol) structure gets paid, and raises the odds a pinned/range (short-vol) structure gets run over.
Funding is stretched positive — what does that mean?
Longs are paying shorts a lot to stay long — the perp market is crowded long. That's a classic setup for a long squeeze (sharp move down as crowded longs get flushed), which cuts against range-bound, short-premium structures on their downside.
Funding is stretched negative — what does that mean?
The mirror case — shorts are crowded and paying longs. Raises the odds of a short squeeze (sharp move up), which is exactly what hurts an uncapped short call (call ratio spread, call side of a ladder) the most.
Put/Call OI ratio is lopsided — what does that mean?
Open interest is piled heavily on one side of the chain. That's positioning, not price — it doesn't say which way price goes, only that a move against the crowded side would be sharper than usual because of forced unwinding. Treated here purely as a risk flag on short-premium, capped-zone structures.
Mempool is congested (BTC only) — does that matter for options?
Only as a loose proxy for on-chain urgency/activity. It is the weakest signal on this desk by design — shown for context, not folded hard into any verdict math — because it reflects network usage, not options positioning.

6. How stock (equity) options actually work — and what changes per structure

Sign in to view the full stock-options breakdown.

Crypto options on Deribit are European-style, cash-settled, and trade 24/7 — none of that is true for listed equity options. If you build any of these 17 structures on stocks instead of crypto, these mechanics change the real-world risk even though the payoff math in this builder is identical.

Contract size (100 shares)
One listed equity option = 100 shares, not 1. Set the Builder's "Multiplier" field to 100 for stocks (it defaults to crypto's 1-coin sizing) — leaving it wrong makes every dollar figure off by 100x.
American-style exercise & early assignment
Nearly all single-stock options can be exercised any time before expiry, not just at expiry like Deribit's crypto options. Any leg where you're short an option can be assigned early — meaning a multi-leg "non-directional" structure can suddenly become a naked stock position overnight, before you intended to close anything.
Dividend / ex-dividend risk on short calls
A short call deep ITM going into an ex-dividend date is a real early-assignment risk — the holder may exercise to capture the dividend, handing you a short-stock position you didn't plan for. This specifically threatens the short-call leg in straddles, strangles, condors, butterflies, call ratio spreads, ladders, and jade lizards.
Margin / buying power
Crypto options on this desk assume you simply hold the premium; equity options require your broker's margin methodology (Reg T or portfolio margin) to even open many of these multi-leg or short-premium structures, and "max loss" in the builder is not the same number as the margin/buying-power your broker will hold.
Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule
US accounts under $25,000 equity face a 3-day-trade-in-5-business-days limit before being flagged PDT, which can restrict exactly the kind of active entry/exit this desk's signals encourage. Crypto/Deribit has no such rule.
Settlement (T+1)
US equity trades settle T+1 (one business day) as of 2024; options themselves settle the next business day after exercise/assignment. Crypto settles effectively instantly. This affects how fast capital from a closed leg is actually available again, not the payoff math itself.

NEUTRAL VECTOR DESK — Internal Manual VIEW ONLY · NO DOWNLOAD
1. What this desk actually does

Neutral Vector Desk is a payoff builder and signal reader for non-directional options structures — straddles, strangles, condors, butterflies, calendars, ratio spreads, backspreads, ladders, the reverse condor, guts, and the box spread. It does not place trades, does not custody funds, and does not connect to any exchange account. It computes payoff math from strikes and premiums you provide (or pulls live from Deribit), and it reads a small set of live market signals to produce a GO / CAUTION / AVOID read per structure. Every number it shows is either entered by you or fetched live from a named public source — nothing on this desk is estimated or invented.

2. How the scanner scans and picks a verdict

For the selected asset, the scanner runs these fetches in parallel on every refresh:

  1. Pulls the nearest-expiry at-the-money option from Deribit to read implied volatility (mark IV) and current spot price.
  2. Pulls Deribit's historical (realized) volatility series for the same asset.
  3. Subtracts the two: IV − RV = the vol premium. This single number is the backbone of every verdict — positive means options are priced richer than actual recent movement (favors selling premium); negative means they're priced cheaper (favors buying it).
  4. Pulls the 8-hour funding rate on the asset's Deribit perpetual. A reading beyond ±0.05% is flagged "stretched" — one side of the market is crowded.
  5. Pulls the Fear & Greed Index (alternative.me). A reading ≤20 or ≥80 is flagged "extreme."
  6. Pulls the full option chain via Deribit's book-summary endpoint and computes the put/call open-interest ratio and the put-minus-call IV skew across all strikes. A PCR above 1.3 or below 0.6 is flagged "crowded."
  7. For BTC only, pulls mempool backlog and fastest fee from mempool.space as a secondary, low-weight context signal.
  8. Each structure's own verdictFor() rule then checks the subset of these flags that actually matters to its specific failure mode (e.g. a condor cares about crowding and funding stretch because those raise breakout risk against its capped range; a calendar cares about the IV/RV spread because it's priced off term structure, not crowding) and returns GO, CAUTION, or AVOID with the specific numbers that drove the call.
  9. The Prediction Radar runs the same inputs through one combined read instead of 17 separate ones — useful as a sanity check when a single structure's verdict looks out of step with the broader picture.

The exact thresholds behind every call are listed in full in Vocabulary & Academy → section 2 — nothing here is a hidden model.

For crypto options specifically, Deribit alone is enough — it is the deepest options book in the asset class and the only one this desk needs for its live signals.
